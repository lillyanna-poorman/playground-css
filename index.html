As a designer, why do I even bother investing learning to code?

First off, let's clarify, while I can both code and design, I that does not mean I am both a developer and designer. The defining difference is that while I can code and enjoy the satisfaction of buiding something on my own, I do not enjoy the process of coding; I like the prize, not the race. Design is different, I love the run, the training, the talk as well as the end result. Because of that, I focus my primary resources and offer my primary skills as a designer.

With that, in the age of AI, why bother learning CSS and coding? Designers should be able to vibe code, right? Such a mindset is like asking a ballet dancer to use a chainsaw. AI is a powerful too, but put in the hands of someone unfamiliar with not only coding practices, basic digital safety could result in inefficency and even unattended consequences. Thinking like a coder involves knowing what decisions make it easier for humans to read, how code can be written for certain team needs, and what to do when AI cannot understand the prompt or intended request.

I do not claim to know everything or be an experience coder. However, I do believe that understanding to basics of coding is important to my personal career and offer a willingness to learn in this area. Main benfits include:

  • Frontend relief for coders focused on backend or API development
  • Better communication with development teams
  • Design understanding that considers technical constraints
